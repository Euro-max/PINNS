What could make your work Publishable:

1. Positioning as Original Contribution:
The manuscript needs to frame exactly what is novel compared to existing PINN + MPC research in CAVs. For example:

Are you the first to integrate PINN with real-time closed-loop MPC on full lateral and longitudinal vehicle dynamics?

Are you offering a new loss formulation, new architecture, or improved performance metrics? This must be explicitly stated and benchmarked.



2. Experimental Validation:

Right now, the results seem to be limited to simulations. For serious journals, including those indexed in Scopus or IEEE Xplore, you'll need either:
a) Comparison against baseline methods across multiple scenarios.
b) Hardware-in-the-loop simulation or limited real-world testing.



3. Writing Style and Structure:

The draft feels like a well-written student project or technical report. Journals expect clearer, shorter abstracts, well-defined sections like Introduction, Related Work, Methodology, Results, Conclusion.

Simplify repetitive content and clarify contributions in the abstract and conclusion.



4. Figures and Tables:

Add clear, properly labeled figures showing key simulation results.

Add summarized tables comparing MPC vs. PINC in a compact way.



5. Referencing:

Format references using the target journal’s style guide. Ensure all references are up-to-date and relevant.

Verify that every cited work is linked to a specific point in the text.
